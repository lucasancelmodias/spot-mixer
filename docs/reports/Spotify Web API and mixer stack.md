# Spotify's 2026 restrictions reshape spot-mixer's design

A Spotify app registered in late October 2026 starts in **Development Mode**. An individual developer cannot move it out, because Extended Quota now admits only organizations with **at least 250k monthly active users**. Development Mode allows **five allowlisted users**, needs the owner's **active Premium**, and shares one unpublished request quota across the developer's whole account. It also removed **`GET /artists/{id}/top-tracks` and every `popularity` field**, so no Spotify endpoint can rank an artist's most-played tracks for feature (a). Feature (b) survives almost intact: top artists and tracks over three time ranges, recent plays, follows, saved items and the new `POST /me/playlists` write path all work. Outside sources fill the gap: Deezer supplies keyless top-track lists, a Spotify `isrc:` search maps them back, and the user's Extended Streaming History export adds lifetime play counts. The Developer Terms ban indefinite caching, ML ingestion and derived listenership profiles, and they require deletion within five days of a disconnect. The stack baseline is Bun 1.4.4, SvelteKit 3.1 with the official `@sveltejs/adapter-bun` 1.0, TypeScript pinned to 6.0.3, and Redis 8.10.2 through Bun's built-in client. Together, these facts make spot-mixer a non-commercial personal tool built around a provider chain, an ISRC-based track matcher and a quota-aware Spotify client.

## Development Mode caps the app at five users and six-month logins

Spotify made six restrictive changes between November 2024 and July 2026, plus two field changes, and **every one binds a Client ID created today**. Existing Extended Quota apps escaped the endpoint cuts. On 2026-03-09, Spotify also postponed the endpoint cuts for existing Development Mode integrations ([Spotify blog, 2026-02-06, with March 9 update](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)). Neither exemption covers a new app. The pace is itself a design input: Spotify announced the February 2026 rules on 2026-02-06 and enforced them for new Client IDs five days later.

No restriction followed. The only later developer post, on 2026-08-13, introduced the Soloist client and changed no Web API rule ([Spotify for Developers news](https://developer.spotify.com/community)). The changelog list still ends at July 2026 ([July 2026 changelog](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/references/changes/july-2026)).

**Development Mode admits up to five authenticated users**, and each one must be on the app's allowlist ([Quota modes](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes)). Developers add users in the Developer Dashboard with the email of their Spotify account ([Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Spotify-403-User-not-registered-in-the-Developer-Dashboard/td-p/5395902)). The cap was 25 from 2021 until the February 2026 change ([Spotify blog, 2021](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2021-05-27-improving-the-developer-and-user-experience-for-third-party-apps); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/06/spotify-changes-developer-mode-api-to-require-premium-accounts-limits-test-users/)). A user outside the allowlist can still finish the login, but every API call with that token returns 403 ([Quota modes](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes)). Developers see the text "Check settings on developer.spotify.com/dashboard, the user may not be registered." ([navispot #1](https://github.com/betsha1830/navispot/issues/1); [rspotify #550](https://github.com/ramsayleung/rspotify/issues/550)). The owner must keep **an active Premium subscription**. When it lapses, calls fail with 403 "Active premium subscription required for the owner of the app", and service can take hours to return after a renewal ([rspotify #550](https://github.com/ramsayleung/rspotify/issues/550); [cowlic #147](https://github.com/kolenkainc/cowlic/issues/147)). Since 2026-07-23, an account can hold 25 Client IDs, but **all of them share one quota** ([Spotify blog, 2026-07-23](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-07-23-web-api-quota-updates)). No source says whether the owner occupies one of the five seats.

The profile endpoint also went quiet. `GET /me` no longer returns `email`, `country`, `product` or `explicit_content` to a Development Mode app ([migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)). The app therefore cannot check a user's Premium status, market or explicit-content preference, and it cannot show the email that the allowlist needs. In May 2026 the endpoint gained `account_id`, which Spotify recommends over `id` for linking accounts ([May 2026 changelog](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/references/changes/may-2026)).

**Extended Quota Mode is closed to spot-mixer.** Since 2025-05-15, Spotify accepts applications "from organizations (not individuals)" ([Quota modes](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes)). An applicant needs a legally registered business, a launched service, at least 250k MAU, presence in key markets and a company email, and review takes up to six weeks ([Quota modes](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes)). The prize is real: unlimited users, no allowlist, a higher rate limit, and endpoints and fields that "remain unchanged" ([Quota modes](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes); [migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)). Pages that still mention a self-service "Request Extension" link predate the organizations-only rule ([Apps](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/apps)). Spreading users across several Client IDs gains nothing: the quota is shared, and Terms VI.1.2 allows one Security Code per app ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). Spotify itself says Development Mode "should not be relied on as a foundation for building or scaling a business on Spotify" ([Spotify blog, 2026-02-06](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)).

**Authorization narrowed to code flows on HTTPS.** Spotify supports three flows: Authorization Code, Authorization Code with PKCE, and Client Credentials ([Authorization](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/authorization)). The implicit grant ended on 2025-11-27 ([Implicit grant](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/implicit-flow)). For a server that can keep its secret, Spotify calls the Authorization Code flow "the recommended choice", and it positions PKCE for clients that cannot store a secret ([Authorization](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/authorization)). Redirect URIs must use HTTPS, except loopback IP literals such as `http://127.0.0.1:PORT` and `http://[::1]:PORT`; **`localhost` is not allowed** ([Redirect URIs](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/redirect_uri)). Matching is exact, but a loopback URI registered without a port accepts any port in the request ([Redirect URIs](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/redirect_uri)). Developers who hit "INVALID_CLIENT: Invalid redirect URI" fixed it by moving to `127.0.0.1` ([n8n #25805](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/issues/25805)). A browser does not send `localhost` cookies to `127.0.0.1`, so the dev site must also open on the loopback IP, or the OAuth `state` cookie is lost.

**Logins now expire after six months.** Access tokens last one hour ([Refreshing tokens](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/refreshing-tokens)). Refresh tokens of new apps expire six months after the user's original authorization, and a refresh does not extend that window ([Spotify blog, 2026-06-18](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-06-18-refresh-token-expiration)). An expired token returns 400 `invalid_grant`, and Spotify tells apps to discard it and send the user through sign-in again, without retries ([Spotify blog, 2026-06-18](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-06-18-refresh-token-expiration)). Tokens carry no issue date, so the app must record its own authorization time. A refresh response does not always include a new refresh token, so the app keeps the old one unless a new one arrives ([Refreshing tokens](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/refreshing-tokens)). Client Credentials tokens are unaffected. Scope requests face a rule too: "Don't ask for scopes on the basis that your app might include a feature later that requires them" ([Compliance Tips](https://developer.spotify.com/compliance-tips)). That rule defers `ugc-image-upload` until a cover feature ships. It also argues for dropping `user-read-private` and `user-read-email`, because the profile fields they gate no longer reach a Development Mode app ([Profile reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/get-current-users-profile)).

## Spotify dropped artist top tracks but kept user listening data

The February 2026 migration guide is the map for a new app. It removed batch reads, artist top tracks, browse categories, new releases, markets and other-user endpoints. It renamed playlist `/tracks` paths to `/items`, moved library writes to `/me/library`, and cut the Search page size from 50 to 10 ([migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)). The 2024 cuts still block related artists, recommendations, audio features and Spotify-owned playlists ([Spotify blog, 2024-11-27](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2024-11-27-changes-to-the-web-api)). A full scan of Spotify's official OpenAPI file agrees with both sources. It flags every removed path as `deprecated: true`, and it leaves the core paths that spot-mixer needs unflagged, including Search, Artist's Albums, top items, saved tracks, `/me/playlists` and `/playlists/{id}/items` ([OpenAPI spec](https://developer.spotify.com/reference/web-api/open-api-schema.yaml)).

**Fields vanished as well.** For Development Mode, Spotify removed track `popularity`, `available_markets` and `linked_from`, album `popularity`, `label`, `album_group` and `available_markets`, and artist `popularity` and `followers` ([migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)). It restored `external_ids` (ISRC and UPC) in March 2026 ([March 2026 changelog](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/references/changes/march-2026)). Artist `genres` is not on the removal list, but the reference marks it deprecated, and one detailed report found no `genres` key at all on `/me/top/artists` for a new app ([spotify-mcp-server #73](https://github.com/marcelmarais/spotify-mcp-server/issues/73)). A comparison of the reference pages with the changelog shows that "Deprecated" now means "absent" for a new app. Spotify tells developers to handle removed fields "being absent, for example by checking for undefined or null" ([migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)).

**For feature (a), the loss is total.** Top tracks return 403 "Forbidden" for new apps, and other developers report the same block ([uc-intg-spotify #22](https://github.com/mase1981/uc-intg-spotify/pull/22); [waxon #39](https://github.com/danfry1/waxon/pull/39)). Without `popularity`, the app cannot even rank an album's tracks by plays. The album path still works, but with smaller pages. Artist's Albums rejects a `limit` above 10 with 400 "Invalid limit", a change that no changelog announced ([waxon #39](https://github.com/danfry1/waxon/pull/39); [Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Unannounced-endpoint-request-limit-change/td-p/7476680)). An artist with 120 releases now costs 12 listing calls. Album-track listings return simplified tracks without ISRCs ([Album tracks reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/get-an-albums-tracks)), and batch reads are gone, so ISRC-based deduplication of album sources costs one `GET /tracks/{id}` per track. The loss of `album_group` has a quieter effect. A request that mixes several `include_groups` values can no longer label the group of each result, so the app should request one group per listing. That point is an inference from the field list, not a documented rule.

**For feature (b), the reads survive.** `GET /me/top/{type}` returns up to 50 artists or tracks per call for `short_term` (about 4 weeks), `medium_term` (about 6 months) and `long_term` (about 1 year) ([Top items reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/get-users-top-artists-and-tracks)). Developers report that offsets of 50 or more return nothing, so the app should plan on 50 items per range ([Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Bug-when-using-offset-greater-than-49-when-pulling-top-tracks/m-p/5581179/highlight/true)). Recently played reaches only the latest 50 plays ([Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/before-and-after-are-utterly-useless-with-me-player-recently/td-p/5520713)). A detailed report confirms that `/me/top/artists` still returns 200 for a new app, minus the removed fields ([spotify-mcp-server #73](https://github.com/marcelmarais/spotify-mcp-server/issues/73)). Top and saved tracks are full track objects, and the reference lists ISRCs on them after the March revert. No report from a new app confirms those ISRCs yet. Genres are unreliable, so the curated mix cannot cluster artists by genre.

**Playlist writes work only on the new paths.** `POST /me/playlists` creates the playlist, and its `public` flag **defaults to `true`**. A private mix must therefore send `public: false` and hold `playlist-modify-private` ([Create playlist reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/create-playlist)). `POST /playlists/{id}/items` appends up to 100 URIs per call, in order, and a JSON body avoids URL-length limits ([Add items reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/add-items-to-playlist)). The old `POST /users/{id}/playlists` returns 403 "You cannot create a playlist for another user", and the old `/tracks` paths return 403 ([Pipedream #20843](https://github.com/PipedreamHQ/pipedream/issues/20843); [cliamp #54](https://github.com/bjarneo/cliamp/issues/54)). The app can read back only playlists that the user owns or collaborates on ([Get playlist reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/get-playlist)). Playlist rows now hold the track under `item`, and one report shows the legacy `track` key holding a boolean ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/iam-tech/your-spotify-playlist-reader-isnt-broken-its-reading-a-boolean-2lml)).

**Market handling changed quietly.** With a user token, the account's country decides availability. A Client Credentials token needs an explicit `market`, or the content counts as "unavailable for the client" ([Get Track reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/get-track)). `GET /me` no longer reveals the country, so shared catalog caches that use the app token need a configured market.

**The official SDK lags the API.** `@spotify/web-api-ts-sdk` 1.2.0 dates from 2024-01-17, and its playlist methods still call the removed `/tracks` and `/users/{id}/playlists` paths ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@spotify/web-api-ts-sdk); [PlaylistsEndpoints.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/spotify-web-api-ts-sdk/main/src/endpoints/PlaylistsEndpoints.ts)). An issue about the resulting 403 errors has no maintainer reply ([ts-sdk #159](https://github.com/spotify/spotify-web-api-ts-sdk/issues/159)). Spotify's OpenAPI 3.0.3 file, by contrast, already holds the 2026 paths and the 10-item limits ([OpenAPI spec](https://developer.spotify.com/reference/web-api/open-api-schema.yaml)). A thin `fetch` client typed from that file, with every deprecated field optional, is the safer base.

## An unpublished account-wide quota and strict Terms bound every call

### Quota lockouts last hours, not seconds

Spotify enforces two separate limits and publishes numbers for neither. The rate limit counts calls in "a rolling 30 second window", and its 429 responses "normally" carry `Retry-After` in seconds ([Rate limits](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/rate-limits)). The Development Mode quota is a separate mechanism ([Rate limits](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/rate-limits)). Since 2026-07-23, it counts all Development Mode apps of one developer account against a single budget, and its 429 body carries `"reason": "QUOTA_EXCEEDED"` ([July 2026 changelog](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/references/changes/july-2026)). Spotify warns that "the specific groupings and limits are subject to change" ([Quota modes](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes)).

Field reports show what the quota feels like. One app made about 17,000 calls a day, ran out every day between about 10:10 and 10:50 (UTC-5), and recovered at 11:04 ([uc-intg-spotify #19](https://github.com/mase1981/uc-intg-spotify/pull/19)). That pattern suggests a daily budget with a fixed reset, but it comes from a single report. One quota 429 carried a `Retry-After` of 20,335 seconds, about 5.6 hours ([Lucky #2897](https://github.com/LucasSantana-Dev/Lucky/pull/2897)). Another project cites cooldowns of 13 to 18 hours, without a source ([spotify-playlist-curator #6](https://github.com/KJ-11/spotify-playlist-curator/pull/6)). Retries do not clear the block; they only drain the shared pool further ([spotify-mcp-cloudflare #2](https://github.com/jamiew/spotify-mcp-cloudflare/issues/2)). spotDL users saw 86,400-second blocks in January and February 2026, and nobody found the cause ([spotDL #2585](https://github.com/spotDL/spotify-downloader/issues/2585); [spotDL #2618](https://github.com/spotDL/spotify-downloader/issues/2618)).

**For spot-mixer, normal use is cheap and bugs are expensive.** A four-artist mix with ten popular tracks per artist costs about 40 `isrc:` searches plus two playlist calls. A curated-mix analysis costs about a dozen calls. Both sit far below the level at which the reported app ran dry. The real threat is a defect: a retry loop, a polling timer, an unbounded library scan, or a test suite that calls the live API. Any of these can lock out every user and every Client ID of the account for hours, sometimes most of a day. The client therefore needs one shared `blocked-until` key in Redis and a parser that separates rate-limit 429s from `QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. It must never retry a quota error, and it must reject `limit` values above the documented maximums before it sends a call. One developer claims that rejected 400 calls still burn quota ([lowspot #2](https://github.com/cfiorelli/lowspot/issues/2)). Nobody has measured that, but the guard costs nothing. Spotify's own advice is to honor `Retry-After`, make calls lazily on user action, cache, and watch the request graphs in the Dashboard ([Rate limits](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/rate-limits)). The same page still recommends batch endpoints, which a new app no longer has ([migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)).

### Terms v10 forbid hoarding, profiling and ML ingestion

Developer Terms version 10 and the Developer Policy both took effect on 2025-05-15 ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms); [Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). No clause addresses the creation or change of a user's playlists, so the core write path carries no special restriction ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). Five other areas bind the design.

**Caching is allowed only as far as strictly necessary.** Terms IV.3.1 bans "compilations or databases of Spotify Content", asks for reasonable efforts to show "the most up to date data available", and says "Do not store Spotify Content indefinitely" ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). IV.3.2 permits temporary caching of metadata and cover art to improve performance ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). Neither document sets a maximum duration, and Spotify's guidance adds: "Avoid storing data that your app can fetch on the fly through the Spotify API" ([Compliance Tips](https://developer.spotify.com/compliance-tips)). Every Redis key that holds Spotify IDs, metadata or listening data therefore needs a bounded TTL, and the app must refresh data before it shows it. Terms IV.2.4(a) also bars tools that "retrieve, duplicate, or index" Spotify Content ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). That rules out background crawling and cache warming.

**User data must leave with the user.** Terms V.8 requires "a working and easily accessible mechanism to disconnect", and Appendix A 5(c) requires deletion of the user's Spotify Personal Data "within five (5) days" ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). Policy I.1 requires a privacy policy that describes how the app uses data ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). Terms V.4 covers the data categories that the user approves at connection. For other personal data, any use beyond "displaying it back to the user" needs explicit consent ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)).

**Analysis and AI are the sharpest limits.** Policy III.13 says "Do not analyze the Spotify Content or the Spotify Service for any purpose", and its examples include "new or derived listenership metrics" and "building profiles of users" ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). Terms IV.2.1(a) bans training on Spotify Content and "otherwise ingesting Spotify Content into a machine learning or AI model" ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). Read literally, III.13 reaches the curated-mix score itself. A per-user artist ranking that the user sees and uses at once resembles "displaying it back" more than profiling, but that is an interpretation, not a Spotify ruling. The safe form computes scores on demand, shows them only to their owner, stores no long-term taste profile and runs no cross-user analytics. No LLM may receive Spotify metadata or listening data, even for a single request.

**Integration with other services is a gray zone.** Policy III.5 bans any product "integrated with streams or content from another service" ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). Terms IV.2.5 says "do not transfer Spotify Content to third parties" ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). No Spotify text says whether a metadata lookup at Deezer or Last.fm counts. The research tracks read it differently. One holds that external ranking data, with no external audio, stays outside III.5. The other treats external play counts that steer track choice as a real risk. Both agree on the safe boundary: never play or show external audio, never send a user's Spotify data out, and never put another brand next to Spotify's ([Design guidelines](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design)).

**Commercial use and branding close the list.** Terms III.1(a) licenses development "for private personal use", and Spotify positions Development Mode for learning, experimentation and personal non-commercial projects ([Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms); [Spotify blog, 2026-02-06](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)). Every displayed track, album, artist or playlist needs Spotify attribution and a link back to Spotify ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). The Spotify logo must never be smaller than 70 px in digital use, and cover art must stay uncropped, with no overlays and a 4 px or 8 px corner radius ([Design guidelines](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design)). Policy III.7 bans any system that can "segue, mix, re-mix, or overlap" Spotify audio, so "mix" must stay a playlist concept with no crossfades ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). Policy III.11 requires "independent value", which cross-artist weighting, ordering and visible reasons can supply ([Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)). The guidelines also bar app names that contain or resemble "Spotify", so the name "spot-mixer" deserves a check before anyone outside the allowlist sees it ([Design guidelines](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design)).

## Deezer, MusicBrainz and the user's own export fill the gaps

**Deezer is the lowest-friction popularity source.** `GET /artist/{id}/top` needs no key, and on 2026-10-25 it returned up to 99 ranked tracks for Daft Punk ([Deezer probe](https://api.deezer.com/artist/27/top?limit=100)). The list items carry no ISRC, so each track needs one `GET /track/{id}` call, which adds `isrc`, `bpm` and `release_date` ([Deezer track probe](https://api.deezer.com/track/3135556)). The lists mix versions: the Daft Punk top 10 held two "Get Lucky" ISRCs and two "One More Time" ISRCs ([Deezer probe](https://api.deezer.com/artist/27/top?limit=10)). Deezer does not define its `rank` value in any document that loaded. It also publishes no quota that the research could read; a third-party proxy cites 50 requests per 5 seconds per IP ([Rocksky README](https://next.tangled.org/rocksky.app/rocksky/tree/main/deezer)). Its terms limit use to non-commercial purposes, forbid association with any trademark or logo, and let Deezer "modify, restrict or remove" access at any time ([Deezer terms](https://developers.deezer.com/termsofuse)).

**Last.fm and ListenBrainz add listening counts, with friction.** Last.fm `artist.getTopTracks` returns tracks "ordered by popularity" with `playcount` and `listeners`, but no ISRC, and it needs an API key ([Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/api/show/artist.getTopTracks)). Its terms add non-commercial use, a "powered by AudioScrobbler" button, a 100 MB storage cap, a ban on its images, and written approval for public pages ([Last.fm API TOS](https://www.last.fm/api/tos)). ListenBrainz's per-artist top-recordings endpoint now returns 401 without a token, citing "bad actors and AI scrapers" ([ListenBrainz probe](https://api.listenbrainz.org/1/popularity/top-recordings-for-artist/056e4f3e-d505-4dad-8ec1-d04f521cbb56)). Its bulk popularity endpoints, which rank lists of MusicBrainz IDs, still answer without one ([ListenBrainz POST probe](https://api.listenbrainz.org/1/popularity/artist)). ReccoBeats returns a `popularity` value for a Spotify track ID, but its origin and freshness are undocumented, so it can serve only as a weak tie-breaker ([ReccoBeats probe](https://api.reccobeats.com/v1/track?ids=0DiWol3AO6WpXZgp0goxAV)). A Spotify-only fallback also exists: search `artist:"<name>"` with `type=track`, then keep results whose artist ID matches ([uc-intg-spotify #22](https://github.com/mase1981/uc-intg-spotify/pull/22)). Spotify does not document that ranking, so it approximates relevance, not plays.

**Matching back to Spotify runs through ISRCs.** The current Search reference and the OpenAPI file both list an `isrc` filter for track searches ([Search reference](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/reference/search); [OpenAPI spec](https://developer.spotify.com/reference/web-api/open-api-schema.yaml)). No developer report yet confirms that the filter works for a new Development Mode app, so it is the first thing to test. ISRCs do not map one-to-one. Several Spotify tracks can share one ISRC, and labels issue new ISRCs for radio edits, remasters and reissues ([Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Inconsistency-in-audio-analysis-for-tracks-with-same-ISRC/td-p/5520935); [DEV Community: matching 90M tracks](https://dev.to/christiaanrc/matching-90m-music-tracks-across-six-platforms-isrcs-fuzzy-matching-and-what-breaks-1hn)). A matcher that covers more than 90 million tracks normalizes case, diacritics and "feat." credits, strips decoration tokens, and tries exact matches before fuzzy ones. It breaks ties on duration and never merges artists on name alone ([DEV Community: matching 90M tracks](https://dev.to/christiaanrc/matching-90m-music-tracks-across-six-platforms-isrcs-fuzzy-matching-and-what-breaks-1hn)). Deezer also resolves an ISRC directly through `GET /track/isrc:<code>` ([Deezer ISRC probe](https://api.deezer.com/track/isrc:GBDUW0000059)).

**MusicBrainz bridges identities, and Odesli is closed.** MusicBrainz resolves Spotify artist, album and track URLs to its own IDs, and an artist lookup lists the matching Deezer and Last.fm links ([MusicBrainz URL probe](https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/url?resource=https://open.spotify.com/track/0DiWol3AO6WpXZgp0goxAV&inc=recording-rels&fmt=json); [MusicBrainz artist probe](https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/artist/056e4f3e-d505-4dad-8ec1-d04f521cbb56?inc=url-rels&fmt=json)). It allows one request per second per IP with a descriptive User-Agent, and its core data is CC0 ([MusicBrainz rate limiting](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API/Rate_Limiting); [MusicBrainz data license](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/About/Data_License)). Daft Punk has two Deezer artist IDs there, so any Deezer-to-Spotify artist mapping needs validation ([MusicBrainz artist probe](https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/artist/056e4f3e-d505-4dad-8ec1-d04f521cbb56?inc=url-rels&fmt=json)). Odesli no longer serves keyless calls: they return 401 `PUBLIC_API_ACCESS_DEPRECATED` ([Odesli probe](https://api.song.link/v1-alpha.1/links?url=https%3A%2F%2Fopen.spotify.com%2Ftrack%2F0DiWol3AO6WpXZgp0goxAV&userCountry=US)).

**Discovery needs similar-artist data from outside Spotify.** Last.fm `artist.getSimilar` gives a `match` score from 0 to 1 and needs an API key ([Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/api/show/artist.getSimilar)). Deezer `/artist/{id}/related` needs no key but gives no score ([Deezer probe](https://api.deezer.com/artist/27/related?limit=2)). ListenBrainz's labs `similar-artists` endpoint and its LB Radio endpoint both answered without a token ([ListenBrainz labs probe](https://labs.api.listenbrainz.org/similar-artists/json?artist_mbids=056e4f3e-d505-4dad-8ec1-d04f521cbb56&algorithm=session_based_days_7500_session_300_contribution_5_threshold_10_limit_100_filter_True_skip_30); [LB Radio probe](https://api.listenbrainz.org/1/lb-radio/artist/056e4f3e-d505-4dad-8ec1-d04f521cbb56?mode=easy&max_similar_artists=3&max_recordings_per_artist=1&pop_begin=0&pop_end=100)). The main ListenBrainz API docs do not list either one, so both are best-effort sources ([ListenBrainz API index](https://listenbrainz.readthedocs.io/en/latest/users/api/index.html)).

**The user's own export is the deepest signal.** Spotify's Extended Streaming History covers "the lifetime of your account", with 21 fields per play, including the Spotify track URI, milliseconds played, IP address and user agent ([Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/understanding-my-data/)). It needs no matching and costs no API quota. The user must request it through "Download your data" on the account privacy page and then upload the JSON files ([Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/data-rights-and-privacy-settings/)). Some records carry null track data, and one account held more than 7,000 such records ([Spotify Community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Other-Podcasts-Partners-etc/Extended-Streaming-History-missing-track-metadata/td-p/6269152)). The Spotify pages that the research could read give no delivery time. For users who scrobble, Last.fm `user.getTopArtists` covers periods from `7day` to `overall` with only a username and an API key ([Last.fm](https://www.last.fm/api/show/user.getTopArtists)). ListenBrainz user statistics need no token, but they can be weeks old and return 204 until computed ([ListenBrainz statistics](https://listenbrainz.readthedocs.io/en/latest/users/api/statistics.html)).

**Audio features stay a later option.** ReccoBeats accepts Spotify track IDs without a key and returns tempo, key, energy and ISRC ([ReccoBeats probe](https://api.reccobeats.com/v1/audio-features?ids=0DiWol3AO6WpXZgp0goxAV)). Its terms allow commercial use, but they say its core metadata is "aggregated from Spotify data", and they promise no availability ([ReccoBeats terms](https://reccobeats.com/docs/documentation/terms-of-service)). Deezer `bpm` was zero for 4 of the 10 Daft Punk top tracks, and AcousticBrainz stopped collecting data in 2022 ([Deezer probe](https://api.deezer.com/artist/27/top?limit=10); [AcousticBrainz](https://acousticbrainz.org/)).

| Need | First choice | Fallbacks | Credentials | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Artist popularity | Deezer `/artist/{id}/top`, then `/track/{id}` for ISRCs | Last.fm `artist.getTopTracks`; Spotify `artist:` search | None for Deezer; key for Last.fm | Non-commercial terms; Spotify III.5 gray zone |
| Mapping to Spotify | Spotify `isrc:` search | Normalized `track:` and `artist:` search; MusicBrainz URL relations | Spotify token | ISRC variants; filter untested on a new app |
| Similar artists | Last.fm `artist.getSimilar` | Deezer `/artist/{id}/related`; ListenBrainz labs and LB Radio | Key; none | Labs endpoints undocumented |
| Listening depth | Extended Streaming History upload | Last.fm `user.getTopArtists`; ListenBrainz statistics | None; key; none | Export holds IP and user-agent data |
| Audio features (later) | ReccoBeats | Deezer `bpm`; AcousticBrainz | None | Spotify-derived data; no availability promise |
| Avoid | Odesli; ListenBrainz per-artist top recordings | None | Closed; token required | Both return 401 |

The shape of this chain follows from the terms. Deezer and MusicBrainz need no keys, so Spotify stays the only required credential. Last.fm becomes an opt-in for self-hosted use, because its public pages need written approval. Data flows inward by default. The app sends Deezer only an artist name, and it sends Spotify only ISRCs, titles and artist names. The riskier direction, which sends Spotify IDs or user data to ReccoBeats or MusicBrainz, stays optional. No source rules on either direction, so the narrowest outbound data is the smallest exposure.

### Bun 1.4 is a required upgrade

Bun 1.4.4, released on 2026-10-22, is current ([Bun release](https://github.com/oven-sh/bun/releases/tag/bun-v1.4.4)). Bun 1.4 rewrote the runtime from Zig to Rust and targets Node.js 26 compatibility. It also makes new workspaces use the isolated linker and makes `bunfig.toml` parsing strict ([Bun 1.4 blog](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4)). The developer machine's Bun 1.2.12 dates from May 2025 and cannot run the official SvelteKit adapter, which requires Bun 1.4 ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun)). Bun 1.4's `bun init` writes TypeScript `^7` ([Bun 1.4 blog](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4)). SvelteKit 3.1, svelte-check and typescript-eslint all declare peer ranges that exclude TypeScript 7, so the project must pin 6.0.3, the newest 6.x release ([npm: typescript](https://www.npmjs.com/package/typescript); [npm registry: @sveltejs/kit](https://registry.npmjs.org/@sveltejs%2Fkit/latest)). `Bun.serve` also closes idle connections after 10 seconds by default ([Bun HTTP server](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/http/server)). That default matters for slow mix builds.

### SvelteKit 3 adds an official Bun adapter and an HTTPS assumption

SvelteKit 3.0 shipped on 2026-10-01, and 3.1.0 followed on 2026-10-14 ([SvelteKit releases](https://github.com/sveltejs/kit/releases)). It requires Vite 8, vite-plugin-svelte 7 and TypeScript 6, moves its config into `vite.config.ts`, and renames the `$lib` alias to `#lib` ([Svelte blog](https://svelte.dev/blog/sveltekit-3-is-here); [SvelteKit 3.0.0 release](https://github.com/sveltejs/kit/releases/tag/%40sveltejs/kit%403.0.0)). The official `@sveltejs/adapter-bun` 1.0.0 builds with `bun run --bun build` and starts with `bun ./build` ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun)). It reads `PORT`, `BODY_SIZE_LIMIT` (default 512K) and `CONNECTION_IDLE_TIMEOUT` (0 to 255 seconds), and it exposes no WebSocket or TLS settings ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun)). The community `svelte-adapter-bun` is deprecated and its repository is archived, although Bun's own SvelteKit guide still recommends it ([npm: svelte-adapter-bun](https://www.npmjs.com/package/svelte-adapter-bun); [Bun SvelteKit guide](https://bun.com/guides/ecosystem/sveltekit)).

**The adapter's origin handling is the main trap.** SvelteKit 3 removed the runtime `ORIGIN` variable. Without `paths.origin`, which is fixed at build time, or a `PROTOCOL_HEADER` from a trusted proxy, the adapter assumes HTTPS ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun)). Plain-HTTP self-hosting without a proxy then fails form POSTs with 403 "Cross-site POST form submissions are forbidden", and a maintainer calls the build-time design deliberate ([SvelteKit #17328](https://github.com/sveltejs/kit/issues/17328)). A local production-like run on `http://127.0.0.1:3000` therefore needs its own build with `paths.origin`. An HTTPS deployment behind a proxy works with forwarded headers.

**A separate API framework is optional.** SvelteKit server routes and hooks can run the OAuth flow, keep tokens away from the browser and talk to Redis. An SPA built with `adapter-static` plus a separate Bun API also works, but it gives up server rendering and server routes, and SPA mode has "a large negative performance impact" ([SvelteKit SPA docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/single-page-apps)). If a separate API stays, Hono 4.13.14 is the steadier choice. Hono 5 is a release candidate that goes ESM-only and moves the Bun adapter into `@hono/bun`, which already supports Hono 4.13.9 and newer ([Hono migration guide](https://github.com/honojs/hono/blob/v5/docs/MIGRATION.md); [npm: @hono/bun](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@hono/bun)). Elysia 2 is also a release candidate, with many breaking changes ([Elysia 2 beta](https://elysiajs.com/blog/elysia-20)). Neither framework ships a session store ([Hono cookie helper](https://hono.dev/docs/helpers/cookie); [Elysia cookie](https://elysiajs.com/patterns/cookie)).

### Redis 8.10 runs through Bun's built-in client

Redis Open Source 8.10.2 is the newest release, and the `redis:8.10.2-alpine` image was rebuilt on 2026-10-22 ([Redis releases](https://github.com/redis/redis/releases); [Docker Hub: redis](https://hub.docker.com/_/redis/tags)). Redis 8 is tri-licensed under RSALv2, SSPLv1 or AGPLv3 ([Redis licenses](https://redis.io/legal/licenses/)). Unmodified self-hosting for one app does not resemble the managed-service use that RSALv2 forbids, but that reading is not legal advice. Valkey 9.1.2 under BSD-3-Clause removes the question, and Bun's client also reads `VALKEY_URL` ([Valkey releases](https://github.com/valkey-io/valkey/releases); [Bun Redis docs](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/redis)). Redis recommends RDB plus AOF for data that must survive, with `appendfsync everysec` as the default, and it discourages AOF alone ([Redis persistence](https://redis.io/docs/latest/operate/oss_and_stack/management/persistence/)). The official image has no password variable and leaves a published port "open without a password to anyone who can reach it" ([Redis image docs](https://github.com/docker-library/docs/blob/master/redis/content.md)). A `PING` healthcheck fits, because `PING` returns an error while Redis still loads its data ([Redis PING](https://redis.io/docs/latest/commands/ping/)).

Bun's built-in client has shipped since Bun 1.2.9. It speaks RESP3 to Redis 7.2 or newer, pipelines automatically, reconnects, and offers typed helpers for strings, hashes and sets ([Bun 1.2.9 blog](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.2.9); [Bun Redis docs](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/redis)). It does not support Cluster or Sentinel, and MULTI/EXEC or EVAL need raw `send()` calls ([Bun Redis docs](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/redis)). That is enough for one Redis node that holds sessions, OAuth state, caches and a cooldown key. A small cache and session interface keeps a later client switch cheap. For Node-based clients, the ioredis README now recommends node-redis for new projects ([ioredis README](https://github.com/redis/ioredis)).

### Compose v2.38 already covers the needed features

Docker Compose v5.6.1 is current, and v5.0.0 replaced the v2 line in December 2025 ([Compose releases](https://github.com/docker/compose/releases)). The developer's v2.38 already supports everything spot-mixer needs. That list includes `depends_on` with `condition: service_healthy` and `restart: true`, healthcheck `start_interval`, profiles and optional `env_file` entries ([Compose services reference](https://docs.docker.com/reference/compose-file/services/)). It also includes `develop.watch` ([Compose develop reference](https://docs.docker.com/reference/compose-file/develop/)). The top-level `version:` key is obsolete and only triggers a warning ([Compose version and name](https://docs.docker.com/reference/compose-file/version-and-name/)). Compose secrets mount at `/run/secrets/<name>`, and Docker prefers them over environment variables, which can leak into logs ([Compose secrets](https://docs.docker.com/compose/how-tos/use-secrets/)). SvelteKit's `$env` modules read variables, not files, so the server needs a small reader for those secret files.

On Windows, Linux containers receive file-change events only for files stored in the WSL file system ([Docker WSL best practices](https://docs.docker.com/desktop/features/wsl/best-practices/)). A project on `E:\` should therefore run its dev server natively with `bun --bun run dev`, with only Redis in Compose ([Bun SvelteKit guide](https://bun.com/guides/ecosystem/sveltekit)). Compose Watch is the alternative, but its image must contain `stat`, `mkdir` and `rmdir`, which rules out distroless ([Compose Watch](https://docs.docker.com/compose/how-tos/file-watch/)). Bun's official Dockerfile installs with `bun install --frozen-lockfile`, tests and builds in an intermediate stage, and runs the release stage as the `bun` user ([Bun Docker guide](https://bun.com/guides/ecosystem/docker)). For `adapter-bun`, the final stage needs the `build` directory, `package.json` and production dependencies ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun)). Bun 1.4's `bun prune --production` can trim those dependencies ([Bun 1.4 blog](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4)).

### Tests split between bun test and Vitest

`bun test` suits the server modules. It offers `mock.module()`, and since Bun 1.3.9 a `spyOn(globalThis, "fetch")` spy can restore itself through `using` ([Bun mocks](https://bun.com/docs/test/mocks); [Bun 1.3.9 blog](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.3.9)). It cannot resolve SvelteKit's `$app` modules, an issue open since 2023, so testable server code must not import them ([Bun #5541](https://github.com/oven-sh/bun/issues/5541)). Svelte's docs recommend Vitest for components ([Svelte testing](https://svelte.dev/docs/svelte/testing)). Bun's own Svelte test guide still installs Svelte 4, so it does not apply ([Bun Svelte test guide](https://bun.com/guides/test/svelte-test)). Playwright's maintainers closed Bun support requests as "not planned", although Bun 1.4 claims that `playwright test` works ([Playwright #27139](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/issues/27139); [Bun 1.4 blog](https://bun.com/blog/bun-v1.4)). End-to-end tests should therefore run under Node.js, and SvelteKit 3.1 declares Node.js 22.17 as its floor ([npm registry: @sveltejs/kit](https://registry.npmjs.org/@sveltejs%2Fkit/latest)). The research did not confirm whether Node.js is installed on the dev machine. For lint and format, ESLint 10 with eslint-plugin-svelte plus Prettier with prettier-plugin-svelte is the mature path; Biome's Svelte support is still experimental ([Biome language support](https://biomejs.dev/internals/language-support/)).

## Design spot-mixer as a budgeted, provider-driven personal tool

The facts above settle most open design choices. The table maps each design area to a decision and to the fact that drives it.

| Area | Decision | Driving fact |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | A personal, non-commercial tool for the owner and a few friends on one Client ID; onboarding asks each friend for the email of their Spotify account | Five seats; Extended Quota closed; `email` hidden from `GET /me` |
| Architecture | One SvelteKit 3.1 app on `adapter-bun`; the Spotify client, providers, matcher, cache and mix engine live in framework-free modules without `$app` imports | Official adapter; `bun test` cannot resolve `$app` modules |

**Artist Mix becomes a resolution pipeline.** The user picks artists through Spotify search. For a "popular" source, the server finds the Deezer artist by name, reads its top list, fetches each track's ISRC and resolves it with an `isrc:` search under the user's token. The first resolved tracks also test the artist mapping. If they do not list the chosen Spotify artist ID, the server tries the next Deezer candidate. That check catches namesakes and artists with two Deezer IDs at no extra cost. Album sources skip the chain and use Artist's Albums plus Album Tracks. Deduplication then runs on URI, on ISRC where known, and on normalized title plus primary artist ID. On a cold cache, a four-artist mix costs about 40 Spotify searches and two playlist calls.

**My Mix becomes a transient score.** About a dozen calls fetch three ranges of top artists and tracks, 50 recent plays, followed artists and a capped number of saved-track pages. The server computes each artist's score with its reasons, shows them, and keeps them only for the session. The familiar pool comes from those reads, and the discovery pool comes from the provider chain minus saved tracks. `GET /me/library/contains` can check saved status by URI, but the research did not verify its scope or batch size ([migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/tutorials/february-2026-migration-guide)). An optional history upload deepens the ranking without quota cost. The browser should parse it into per-artist and per-track totals, because `adapter-bun` limits request bodies to 512K by default and the raw files hold IP addresses ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun); [Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/understanding-my-data/)).

**One SvelteKit app beats a split stack, provided the core stays portable.** The strongest case for a separate Bun API plus a static SPA is maturity: Hono 4.13 is stable, and an SPA avoids the adapter's origin trap. Against it stand two dev processes, one Hono version pinned across workspaces for the typed client, and an RPC layer that SvelteKit's own server routes make redundant ([Hono RPC](https://hono.dev/docs/guides/rpc)). SvelteKit 3 is only weeks old, so the domain modules must stay free of framework imports. A later move behind Hono then costs little. The login flow follows Spotify's documented server choice, the confidential Authorization Code flow. The research did not collect Spotify's PKCE parameters, so a PKCE layer on top needs its own test first.

**Two runtime limits need explicit settings.** `Bun.serve` drops idle connections after 10 seconds by default ([Bun HTTP server](https://bun.com/docs/runtime/http/server)). A cold ten-artist build makes about 120 Deezer calls, which take about 12 seconds at the unofficial ceiling of 50 calls per 5 seconds. The preview route should therefore stream progress, or it should raise `CONNECTION_IDLE_TIMEOUT` toward its 255-second cap ([adapter-bun docs](https://svelte.dev/docs/kit/adapter-bun)). Origin handling needs a decision per environment: `paths.origin` in a dedicated local build, and forwarded headers behind the HTTPS proxy that any non-loopback redirect URI needs anyway.

**Five unknowns deserve tests in the first milestone.** No report yet confirms that `isrc:` search works for a new Development Mode Client ID. None confirms that full track objects carry `external_ids.isrc` after the March revert. No source confirms `bun --bun run dev` with SvelteKit 3.1 and Vite 8 on Windows. No source says whether the owner uses one of the five seats. Finally, nobody has measured the Deezer-to-Spotify ISRC hit rate for the artists that users will pick. The Client ID itself should be registered early, because developers reported a pause in new app creation from about late December 2025 ([flutster #1](https://github.com/Nicsilver/flutster/issues/1); [spotDL #2585](https://github.com/spotDL/spotify-downloader/issues/2585)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts spot-mixer's center of gravity. Spotify no longer says what is popular. It now resolves identities, supplies the user's own taste and stores the result. The riskiest component is therefore the track matcher, not the mix engine. Its quality rests on two facts that nobody has confirmed for a new app: a working `isrc:` filter and populated ISRCs. Those two tests come before any interface work.

The binding constraint is blast radius, not throughput. One owner subscription, one account-wide quota and five seats are single points of failure, and one runaway loop can lock out every user for hours, sometimes most of a day. The short-lived, on-demand design that protects the quota also satisfies the Terms on caching, profiling and deletion, so resilience and compliance point the same way. Spotify gave new apps five days' notice in February, changed a page limit without a changelog, and says it will "continue to evolve this model" ([Spotify blog, 2026-02-06](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)). spot-mixer should therefore treat the API contract as runtime data: detect capabilities, cache refusals, and keep every outside provider replaceable.
